ARTICLE                 INFO                                  ABSTRACT

Keywords:                                                     In engineering applications such as service robotics, human–robot collaboration, and industrial automation,
Reinforcement learning                                        robotic manipulators frequently operate in highly dynamic and partially observable environments. The presence
Dynamic environments                                          of dynamic obstacles, unpredictable human behaviors, and rapidly changing task demands intensify the
Robotic manipulators motion planning
                                                              inherent conflicts between positioning accuracy, obstacle avoidance, and operational safety. At the same time,
Trend learning
                                                              these systems must rely on incomplete and noisy sensor data to perceive and interpret their surroundings. Such
Reward shaping
Distillation
                                                              dynamic scenarios significantly increase system complexity and require adaptive, real-time control strategies
                                                              capable of making reliable decisions under uncertainty. To address these challenges, this paper proposes
                                                              Trend Learning – Adaptive Reward Shaping – Temporal Difference Knowledge Distillation of Q-value(Action-
                                                              Value Function), collectively referred to as TL-ARS-TDKDQ, a reinforcement learning framework designed
                                                              to enable robotic manipulators to adaptively perform precise positioning and dynamic obstacle avoidance
                                                              in dynamic environments. Trend Learning(TL) alleviates environmental uncertainty by extracting temporal
                                                              dependencies from sequential data of the manipulator. Adaptive Reward Shaping(ARS) dynamically balances
                                                              positioning accuracy and obstacle avoidance for the robotic manipulator while progressively increasing task
                                                              difficulty via curriculum learning. To enhance stability during reward fluctuations caused by ARS,Temporal
                                                              Difference Knowledge Distillation Q-value (TDKDQ) employs a dynamic teacher network and Temporal
                                                              Difference(TD) error-based balancing, ensuring stable policy convergence in non-stationary scenarios involving
                                                              robotic manipulator control. Experiments with a KUKA arm in CoppeliaSim demonstrate that TL-ARS-TDKDQ
                                                              significantly improves convergence speed, control stability, and task success when integrated into mainstream
                                                              continuous control reinforcement learning algorithms.

                                                                                         5. Conclusion

                                                                                             In this paper, we proposed a novel TL-ARS-TDKDQ framework
                                                                                         for robotic dynamic obstacle avoidance and target localization under
                                                                                         non-stationary environments. The framework integrates Trend Learn-
                                                                                         ing (TL) and a TD-error-based Q-Value Knowledge Distillation (TD-
                                                                                         KDQ) mechanism within an Adaptive Reward Shaping (ARS) scheme.
                                                                                         It systematically tackles critical issues in robotic manipulator multi-
                                                                                         task control for dynamic obstacle avoidance and positioning accuracy
                                                                                         in highly dynamic and partially observable environments, including
                                                                                         decision bias under partially observable Markov decision processes
                                                                                         (POMDPs), conflicts among multiple task objectives, and instability in
Fig. 16. Evolution of per-episode rewards under the Trend Learning mech-                 policy optimization due to non-stationary environmental conditions.
anism with different temporal sequence lengths in adaptive robotic motion                    To validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework, we inte-
planning tasks.                                                                          grated it into six mainstream continuous control reinforcement learn-
                                                                                         ing algorithms and conducted comprehensive comparative experiments
                                                                                         using a 7-DOF KUKA robotic manipulator within the CoppeliaSim
                                                                                         simulation platform. Experimental results demonstrate that, compared
                                                                                         with the original algorithms, the TL-ARS-TDKDQ-integrated methods
                                                                                         achieve significant improvements in policy convergence speed, train-
                                                                                         ing stability, and overall task performance. These findings verify the
                                                                                         generality and effectiveness of the proposed framework for dynamic
                                                                                         obstacle avoidance and localization control in robotic systems under
                                                                                         dynamic multi-task environments.
                                                                                             In future work, we will advance our framework toward real-world
                                                                                         robotic deployment by integrating visual perception modules that fuse
                                                                                         image data with proprioceptive state information, thereby enhancing
                                                                                         environmental awareness and robustness under real sensory noise and
                                                                                         dynamic uncertainty. Moreover, we plan to extend the proposed ap-
                                                                                         proach to human–robot collaborative scenarios in unstructured and
                                                                                         dynamic environments, aiming to achieve safe, efficient, and adaptive
                                                                                         motion planning in practical applications.
